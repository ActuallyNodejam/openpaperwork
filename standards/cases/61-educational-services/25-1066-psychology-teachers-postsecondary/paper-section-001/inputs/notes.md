# Reading notes, related work (sleep + exams)

Plan: go from how common short sleep is -> what it does to memory -> what
pushes sleep later in exam weeks -> whether anything helps. End on the gap
our study fills (nobody has measured sleep and actual exam grades in the
same week with actigraphy).

## wellbeing (survey report 2024)
- national survey, 18,400 students
- 41% report under 7 h sleep on a typical exam-week night
- "short sleep" in the report = self-reported, not measured. Say so.

## haddad (book 2019)
- good framing chapter: sleep is when the day's learning gets stabilised
- argues universities schedule exams as if sleep didn't matter
- it's a book / argument, not new data. Don't cite it for a number.

## nguyen + fischer (chapter 2018)
- review chapter: declarative memory consolidation depends on slow-wave sleep
- lab studies mostly, short word-list tasks. Generalising to exams is a stretch,
  they say so themselves.

## lindqvist et al.
- 96 first-years, actigraphy for 10 nights
- each extra hour of sleep -> 6% better next-day recall on a word-pair test
- correlational! no causal claim

## kowalczyk + tanaka
- exam periods: caffeine intake roughly doubles vs. term weeks
- higher caffeine after 4 pm linked to ~40 min later sleep onset
- sample 310, diaries

## moreau et al.
- diary study, 154 students, 3 weeks
- each hour of screen use in the last hour before bed... no wait: any screen
  use in the hour before bed linked to 14 min longer sleep latency
- effect smaller on weekends

## ruiz
- 5-session sleep-hygiene course during exam weeks, 120 students, randomised
- improved self-reported sleep quality
- BUT only for students who completed at least 4 of 5 sessions. Overall
  effect not significant. Don't overclaim.
- no effect on grades measured
